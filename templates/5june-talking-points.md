# Talking Points — 5 June Presentation
## Addressing the Induna of Osizweni / Flathela

---

## Protocol Notes

When addressing an Induna in a formal setting:

- Open with the correct salutation — **"Ndabezitha"** (for a chief/inkosi) or **"Nkosi"** if addressing the Inkosi directly. For an Induna, use **"Mhlonishwa"** (respected one) or simply **"Nduna"** with respect.
- Do not rush. Allow silence. Speak slowly and clearly.
- Do not open with the platform. Open with the community's situation.
- Always frame the platform as serving the council's authority — not replacing it.
- End by asking for the council's guidance, not their approval. The distinction matters.

---

## Opening (Respect and Context)

> "Mhlonishwa, ngiyabonga ngethuba lokukhuluma nawe namuhla."
> *(Respected one, I am grateful for the opportunity to speak with you today.)*

> "I come before this council not as someone who has answers, but as someone who has built a tool — and I am asking the council to decide whether this tool serves the community."

---

## The Problem (Speak to What They Already Know)

> "The Buffalo River project has started. The sod has been turned. The mayor has spoken. But the community of Mndozo, Manzana, Dicks, Jobstown, and Johnstone is asking questions that no one is answering clearly."

> "How many jobs are there? When do they start? Who decides who gets them? What happens if the contractor brings people from outside?"

> "These are not new questions. They are the same questions that have stopped projects before. They are the questions that cause conflict."

---

## The Platform (Show, Don't Tell)

*[Open the phone or laptop. Show the Buffalo River page.]*

> "This page already exists. It is live. It shows the project, the employer, the funding, the phase, the jobs target, the deliverables, and the media statement from the Mayor."

> "It is linked to the Khathide Traditional Council area. It is visible to every resident with a phone."

> "But right now, it is running without the council's formal authority behind it. I am here to ask the council to make it official."

---

## The Ask (Frame as Protecting the Council)

> "I am not asking the council to trust technology. I am asking the council to use this tool to protect its own authority."

> "If the council adopts this platform, then every project in this area — every development, every notice, every hiring process — must be recorded here. The municipality cannot bypass the council. The contractor cannot make promises they don't keep. The community can see everything."

> "The council's name is on this. The council's oversight is permanent. No one can erase it."

---

## On the CLO Application (If Appropriate to Raise)

> "I have also applied for the Community Liaison Officer position on this project. I am still waiting for the outcome."

> "Whether I am appointed or not, this platform serves the community. If I am appointed, I bring this entire system as part of my service to Ward 7 — at no cost to the council."

> "If someone else is appointed, I will approach them as a partner. The platform makes their job easier. It protects them from accusations of corruption. It proves they are doing their work."

---

## On the 30 vs. 100 Jobs (If Raised)

> "At the sod-turning, 30 workers were confirmed. The target is 100. This is normal for the early phase — site clearance and earthworks come first."

> "But the community does not know this. They see 30 and they think 70 jobs were stolen."

> "With this platform, the council can publish: 'Phase 1 — 30 positions. Phase 2 opens when construction begins. 70 positions remaining. Register with the Induna.' The information gap closes. The conflict does not start."

---

## The Resolution (What You Need Signed)

> "I have prepared a simple resolution — one page. It says the council adopts this platform as its official digital record. It does not give anyone authority over the council. It gives the council authority over the digital record."

> "I have also prepared a letter — from the Induna to the municipality, the engineer, and the contractor. It asks them to share project updates with the platform. It is the council's voice, not mine."

*[Place both documents on the table. Do not push them. Let the council examine them.]*

---

## Closing (Ask for Guidance, Not Approval)

> "Mhlonishwa, I do not ask the council to decide today if you are not ready. I ask only that you look at what has been built, and tell me whether it serves the community."

> "This platform belongs to the council. I am only the person who built it."

> "Ngiyabonga, Ndabezitha."
> *(Thank you, respected one.)*

---

## If Asked About Cost

> "The council pays nothing. The platform is free for the council. Businesses, developers, and contractors who want to use it for their own purposes — they pay. The council's use is always free."

---

## If Asked Who Controls It

> "The council controls everything that is published. Nothing goes on the platform without the council's knowledge. I operate it on the council's behalf — but the council can remove me at any time and the records remain."

---

## If Asked About the Municipality

> "The municipality does not own this. The municipality is a stakeholder — like the contractor, like the engineer. The council is the authority. The platform reflects that."

---

## Key Phrases to Return To

- *"This protects the council's authority."*
- *"The community can see everything."*
- *"The records cannot be erased."*
- *"The council pays nothing."*
- *"I built this to serve the community, not to profit from it."*
